# Model Card: Agri Yield Forecaster (end-of-August, district-crop)

> **All data in this project is SYNTHETIC (generated by src/data_gen.py). Results demonstrate the methodology, not real agronomic findings.**

## 1. Intended use
Forecast final crop yield (quintal/ha) for six Vidarbha districts and seven crops on **31 August**, so a procurement team can secure or avoid supply before harvest. Intended for ranking district-crop pockets and sizing volumes, not for single-field decisions.
**Out of scope:** real-world agronomic claims, insurance or credit decisions, other regions or crops, any use without re-training on real data.

## 2. Data
Synthetic dataset, 350,000 raw rows (about 346,500 after cleaning), 2006-2024, produced by a documented data-generating process (`src/data_gen.py`) with deliberately injected defects: mixed Rs/kg and Rs/quintal prices, missing monsoon rainfall, yield in wrong units, duplicates, spelling errors, outliers.
Synthetic data was used because no real records were available. The ground-truth defect log allows an honest check of the cleaning rules.

## 3. Training / validation / test (time-aware, no random split)
- Train **2008-2018** (200,783 rows); 2006-07 only supply history for lag features.
- Validation **2019-2021** (54,379 rows); Test **2022-2024** (54,433 rows), used once at the end.
- Model selection and tuning used expanding-window CV inside 2008-2018 only. The final model was fitted on train only (not re-fitted on validation).

## 4. Model
`AnchoredRegressor` around a scikit-learn Pipeline (imputer, ordinal encoding, LightGBM). It predicts **yield / normal-yield** and multiplies back by the "normal" (75th percentile of that district-crop's previous 5 seasons). This lets the model learn only weather- and soil-driven deviations and carry the technology trend through the anchor. Best model chosen by CV: **LightGBM**; Optuna (8 trials) lowered CV MAE from 1.368 to 1.359.
Feature set chosen by CV: **A: weather + soil + crop/district** (21 inputs). Adding lag-ratio, price-lag, rolling and per-week rainfall features, or a village feature, did **not** improve CV and was dropped; see the notebook.

### Success criteria
| Criterion | Result | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| (a) Final model beats baseline by >= 15% (MAE) | **PASS** | vs last-year baseline: val +65.3%, test +25.4% | vs stronger 5-season-normal baseline: val +26.6%, test +26.1% |
| (b) Test MAE within 5% of validation MAE | **PASS** | val 1.474 vs test 1.505 q/ha (gap +2.1%) |
| (c) Top-5 SHAP drivers make agronomic sense | **PASS** | cum_rainfall_june_aug (ok); rainfall_vs_normal_ratio (ok); season (ok); soil_organic_carbon_pct (ok); soil_ph (ok) |

**Read the validation gain carefully:** the +65% on validation versus last year's yield is inflated by 2019 (the year after the 2018 drought, last-year baseline MAE 8.8). Against the stronger 5-season-normal baseline the gain is about 26% in both periods. Without the anchor, the same LightGBM on raw yield with 40 features scores 3.14 on validation.

### High-risk detection (yield >15% below own 5-season average, recall at precision >= 70%)
- Out-of-fold CV years that include droughts 2014/2015/2018: recall **99.8%** (last-year baseline 19.6%).
- Test 2022-24 (no drought, positives are noise-driven, 7.8% of rows): recall 32.0%, PR-AUC 0.59. The model is weak at flagging single-field random bad luck.

## 6. Explainability
Top-5 SHAP drivers: Cumulative rain Jun-Aug, Rain vs normal, Season (Kharif/Rabi), Soil organic carbon, Soil pH. Rainfall features dominate (mean |SHAP| 1.99 and 1.51 q/ha), organic carbon is positive, as expected. `season` and `soil_pH` are weaker context features (their agronomic direction is not strongly interpretable), so criterion (c) is a judgment, not a proof.

## 7. Fairness and stability
- Groups checked: district, crop, season, farm size (terciles). Relative error (MAPE) is within 20% of average for every group. **Flagged on absolute MAE only:** Bhandara (district), rice (crop), wheat (crop) (high-yield crops/districts have larger absolute errors). Bias flags (>5%): none.
- Bootstrap 95% CI of test MAE: 1.494-1.517. With 50% of rainfall readings removed MAE rises 3.9%; with 10% rainfall noise 4.2%.
- PSI train vs test: rainfall features show large drift (PSI > 0.25) because the training period contains drought years and the test period does not. This is a regime difference, not a data problem.
- Recalibration (fitted on validation, judged on test): linear -0.1%, isotonic -2.6% MAE. Isotonic recalibration is recommended for deployment but is **not** baked into `final_model.joblib`.

## 8. Leakage controls
Cutoff 31 August. Excluded: rainfall after August, mandi prices at harvest, production (= yield x area), final-season area, full-data target encodings. Lags and rolling statistics are shifted so the current season is never included. Encoders/imputers are fitted inside pipelines on training folds only. The notebook demonstrates the score inflation caused by a leaky feature.

## 9. Known limitations
1. Synthetic data; real weather is not a two-regime (drought/normal) world. Rainfall dominance is an artefact of the generator.
2. Cleaning error: 96.2% precision on kg/ha fixes. Some injected extreme yields were wrongly divided by 100 (about 68 test rows). See `reports/wrong_prediction_case_study.md`. Test MAE is 1.505 on all rows and 1.496 excluding them.
3. The first two years (2006-07) have imputed lag history and are not used for training.
4. Only 11 training seasons and 3 droughts; extrapolation to unseen extremes is untested. The drought procurement table is a **simulation** (weather of past drought years applied to 2024 records), not a forecast.
5. Rows are individual farm records; tonnes in the procurement tables are for these sampled records, not whole-district production.

## 10. Monitoring and retraining
Monitor monthly: MAE by district/crop, mean error (bias), PSI of rainfall and soil features, share of imputed rainfall and unit-fixed yields. Re-train every season after harvest data arrives, and immediately if PSI > 0.25 on non-rainfall features or bias exceeds 5% for any group. Re-check the cleaning rules against any new defect type.

## 11. Ethical considerations
Forecasts should not be used to under-pay farmers or to exclude small farms. Report errors by group, publish intervals, and keep a human in the loop for procurement commitments.
